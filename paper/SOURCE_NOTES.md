# Source versions and scope

The paper credits the incidence construction and its rectangle geometry to Hatami, Hatami, Pires, Tao, and Zhao. Its claim is the resulting learning separation and the stated quantitative and probabilistic extensions, not the invention of that incidence construction.

The full texts used for the requested comparisons were:

- Hatami, Hatami, Pires, Tao, and Zhao, *Lower bound methods for sign-rank and their limitations*, ECCC TR22-079 (2022), including the appendices and open problems.
- Patel, *A Separation Between Distribution-Free SQ Learning and Dimension Complexity*, arXiv:2609.19780v1. This version was posted September 17, 2026. The comparison uses its unpadded Theorem 3; its padding observation supplies a qualitative polynomial-query separation for a natural class.
- Karchmer and Malach, *The Power of Random Features and the Limits of Distribution-Free Gradient Descent*, arXiv:2505.10423v1 and the ICML 2025 paper. Theorem numbering in this revision refers specifically to arXiv v1. Definitions 3.2 and 3.5 and Theorems 3.3 and 4.1 are distinguished explicitly. The counterexample concerns Theorem 4.1 under its written quantifiers, not a claim that Theorem 3.3's conclusion has been disproved.
- Razborov and Sherstov, *The sign-rank of AC0*, SIAM Journal on Computing 39(5):1833-1855 (2010), author-hosted full text.
- Diakonikolas, Kane, and Ren, *Faster Algorithms for Agnostically Learning Disjunctions and Their Implications*, COLT 2025, PMLR 291:1531-1558, and arXiv:2504.15244v1.

Additional structural and expository comparisons used Alon, Moran, and Yehudayoff (COLT 2016), Feldman (COLT 2017), and Kamath, Montasser, and Srebro (COLT 2020), together with the COLT 2025 paper above. Their mathematical ingredients are cited where used. No claim of award status is made.

## Rechecking and novelty

The five named mathematical arguments were reviewed independently of their earlier descriptions, and finite checks were rerun. The new prior-average and transcript proofs are complete in the paper, but have not received an external referee review or whole-paper formal verification. Finite arithmetic tests are supporting checks only. No claim is made that this is the first public identification of a problem with Karchmer and Malach's argument.
