A fixed linear representation must accommodate every label pattern in a class at once. A statistical-query learner instead adapts to the input distribution: it asks where the distribution places its weight, then labels regions on which the remaining candidates agree or eliminates candidates that disagree. The paper builds classes, from randomly signed point-line incidences, that such a learner handles with logarithmically many queries of constant tolerance, while every linear representation needs a number of dimensions linear in the grid scale, which is exponential in the input length. This remains true for approximate and randomized representations, and even when the representation may depend on a prior over targets and ignore a fixed fraction of them. The logarithmic query order is optimal for typical members of the construction. The paper proves no result about gradient descent on a specified network.
