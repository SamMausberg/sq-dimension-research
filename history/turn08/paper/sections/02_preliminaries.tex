\section{Definitions and notation}\label{sec:preliminaries}
Let $X$ be finite and nonempty and let $H=\{h_1,\ldots,h_K\}\subseteq\{-1,+1\}^X$ be a nonempty indexed class. Repeated rows are permitted. Write
\[
 L_{D,h}(g)=\Pr_{x\sim D}[g(x)\ne h(x)],\qquad
 \E_D[hg]=1-2L_{D,h}(g).
\]
All predictors are Boolean unless a score and its threshold rule are specified. Unsubscripted logarithms in asymptotic bounds may use either base.

\paragraph{Oracle and learner.}
A query $q:X\times\{-1,+1\}\to[-1,1]$ receives any $v$ with $|v-\E_Dq(x,h(x))|\le\tau$ \citep{Kearns98}. A valid policy may use the full public query-answer history, including queries that reveal past private choices, but not unrevealed future coins. A depth-$m$ learner makes at most $m$ adaptive queries and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guarantee}
 \forall D\ \forall h\in H\ \forall\text{ valid policies }O,
 \qquad \E_\omega L_{D,h}(A^O_\omega)\le\epsilon.
\end{equation}
The seed is independent of the instance. Early stopping can be padded by zero queries. Randomized policies are handled by conditioning on their randomness. Our finite-state algorithms are measurable. Computation is uncharged unless stated; computational results use finite binary answers and reserve half the tolerance for rounding. Deterministic guarantees are pathwise.

\paragraph{Dimension.}
The dimension complexity $\dc(H)$ is the least $d$ for which one $\phi:X\to\R^d$ and row weights $w_h$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict}
 h(x)\langle w_h,\phi(x)\rangle>0\qquad(x\in X,h\in H).
\end{equation}
It is the sign-rank of the truth table: a rank factorization and a common feature representation are the same object \citep{BDES02}.

For probabilistic dimension fix $\sign(0)=+1$. A single embedding law, independent of $D,h$, witnesses $\dc_{1/2}(H)\le d$ when
\[
 \forall D,h,\quad \Pr_\phi[\exists w:L_{D,h}(\sign\langle w,\phi\rangle)=0]\ge1/2.
\]
It witnesses $\dc^\eta(H)\le d$ when
\[
 \forall D,h,\quad \E_\phi\inf_w L_{D,h}(\sign\langle w,\phi\rangle)\le\eta.
\]
The source attributes these variants to \citet{CMW25} and \citet{KMS20}. Its literal raw-score loss assigns zero loss to $w=0$; the definitions above fix a Boolean tie rule. Finiteness makes the infimum a minimum. \Cref{lem:ties} gives the same lower bounds for the opposite fixed tie rule and when zero scores count as errors.

\paragraph{Rectangles.}
A rectangle $S\times T\subseteq X\times H$ has color $c$ when $h(x)=c$ on it. Define
\[
 \rect(H)=\inf_{\mu\in\Delta(X),\nu\in\Delta(H)}
 \max_{S\times T\text{ monochromatic}}\mu(S)\nu(T).
\]
Restricting a matrix cannot decrease this ratio, by extending weights by zero. \Cref{lem:finite-minimax,lem:product-weights} record the finite separation and rational-weight reductions used below.
